Abstract

Abstract

We propose Structured Source Discrete Diffusion (S2D2), a framework that learns to refine the output of a base model toward the ground-truth distribution on the discrete probability simplex.

Semantic Scene Completion (SSC) predicts dense 3D geometry and semantics from sparse LiDAR observations. Existing methods treat SSC as a discriminative regression problem, and their predictions degrade sharply under extreme sparsity (~1% voxel occupancy) and class imbalance (over 7,000× between the most and least common classes on SemanticKITTI).

Standard diffusion generates from noise. S2D2 instead starts from a supervised base model’s prediction and learns the correction that carries it to the ground truth. The reverse update is closed-form, so a single step is enough at deployment — we do not iterate, and we do not distill a multi-step schedule. We pair this core with coarse-to-fine pyramid diffusion for synthetic data augmentation and a matching BEV conditioning stream.

On the SemanticKITTI hidden test leaderboard, S2D2 reaches 39.2% mIoU under D4 test-time augmentation and 39.0% without — both establish the new LiDAR single-frame state of the art (+1.3% over TALoS, the previous best at 37.9%). The framework is also base-agnostic: applied without architectural change to three structurally different frozen base models, the identical recipe lifts LMSCNet from 12.10% to 16.59% (+4.49 pp), the point-voxel hybrid JS3C-Net from 22.73% to 26.72% (+3.99 pp, official semantic-kitti-api evaluator), and SCPNet from 36.17% to 38.54% (+2.37 pp) — direct evidence the refinement is a property of the framework, not of any specific base. The from-noise variant of S2D2 (uniform source, LiDAR-only conditioning) reaches 30.54% val mIoU without any 3D SSC base — ahead of the strongest non-SCPNet baseline, PaSCo, whose 30.11% is a subsidiary mIoU under its panoptic-mode evaluator and so not a like-for-like comparison; stacked with one S2D2 refinement step it reaches 32.88%. Applied as a secondary task to LiDAR-only BEV perception, S2D2 reaches 36.09% mIoU, 9.06 points above the previous best dedicated 2D method. The 1-step real-time configuration runs at 9.33 FPS marginal cost on a contention-free H100 and still hits 38.8% on test — beating TALoS by +0.9% in real time without TTA.

Method

Refine a base model’s prediction, don’t regenerate the scene from noise.

Standard diffusion turns pure noise into data across many steps. S2D2 instead starts from a pretrained SSC model’s output and learns the correction that carries it to the ground truth. That change — what it starts from, and how many steps it takes — is the whole idea.

01 Start from a real prediction, not noise

Classical diffusion denoises random noise until it looks like the data. S2D2 denoises another model’s prediction toward ground truth.

The base model already captures the coarse scene (road, buildings, vegetation), so the correction task is much smaller and much more forgiving of noisy conditioning.

02 Correct once, then stop

The reverse update has a closed form: the direction from the base prediction to ground truth is exact. We integrate it in a single Euler step.

No iterative rollout, no teacher/student distillation. On SemanticKITTI val, one step matches a 100-step schedule within 0.1 mIoU while adding only 107 ms on an H100.

03 Drop in on top of any SSC model

The refiner reads in the frozen base model’s output alongside a BEV semantic map and the raw LiDAR voxelization, then emits a corrected voxel grid.

Empirically base-agnostic across three structurally different frozen bases: LMSCNet (12.10 → 16.59%, +4.49 pp), point-voxel hybrid JS3C-Net (22.73 → 26.72%, +3.99 pp, under official semantic-kitti-api), and SCPNet (36.17 → 38.54%, +2.37 pp) — same recipe, same hyperparameters, only the source changes.

Architecture in detail 3-layer breakdown: transport intuition · dense UNet · rare-class recovery

Data side · co-equal contribution

A pyramid pipeline that turns long-tail scarcity into supervised pairs.

SemanticKITTI puts more than 7,000× as many vegetation voxels in the training set as motorcyclist voxels. The model side cannot reweight its way out of that — the rare classes simply are not there to learn from. The data side runs offline, once, on top of the existing supervised set: pyramid discrete diffusion synthesises full complete scenes; a quality filter keeps the in-distribution half; an HDL-64E ray-tracer pairs each with a sparse input; a rare-class object bank fills the residual gaps. The result is an extra 32,039 labelled pairs that the rest of the system can train against without any change to the SSC pipeline.

01 Generate the dense scene

Pyramid multinomial diffusion synthesises full 2562×32 labelled scenes from coarse to fine. Each scale conditions on the upsampled prior; the top scale tiles into overlapping 1362×32 patches merged by majority vote.

A Jensen–Shannon divergence filter then drops samples whose class distribution drifts away from real KITTI scenes — only the top half survives.

02 Match it to a real sensor

A simulated Velodyne HDL-64E ray-tracer fires 64 × 2,048 = 131,072 rays per sweep across every synthetic scene, with up to two returns per beam and σr = 3 cm range noise.

Bresenham-3D voxel traversal produces the matching sparse input X̂ at the same density real LiDAR would — so the synthetic pairs look, train, and fail like real ones.

03 Fill the rare tail

A pre-built bank of 57,789 instances across 8 safety-critical rare classes (bicycle, motorcycle, truck, other-vehicle, person, bicyclist, motorcyclist, trunk) pastes onto ground-level voxels of every synthetic scene.

Placement passes three legality checks: in-bounds within the 2562×32 grid, no-collision with existing non-empty voxels of a different class, and grounded on road / sidewalk / parking / terrain.

The pipeline is decoupled from the SSC model: any voxel-grid LiDAR perception task that shares the long-tail pathology can reuse it. We release the 32K synthetic pool alongside the headline checkpoint.

Where the gains concentrate

The improvements land on the safety-critical long-tail. On the SemanticKITTI hidden test set, S2D2 with D4 TTA holds the column-wise maximum on 7 of the 8 safety-critical classes (bicycle, truck, other-vehicle, person, bicyclist, motorcyclist, traffic-sign). Only motorcycle goes to TALoS, by 2.7 points.

Mean per-class gain over SCPNet: +3.58 across the 8 safety-critical classes vs +1.70 across the 11 remaining classes — a 2.1× concentration onto rare safety-critical classes. Common-class regressions stay tiny and stay outside the safety set (motorcycle −0.7, trunk −0.2, pole −1.3). This pattern drives our SC-mIoU and VRU-IoU safety-aware metrics in the paper.
